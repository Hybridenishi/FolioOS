## The irregularity, stated plainly

The work-state contract in force at this moment is kernel 0.3.0, whose rule 1 reads:
"Only a human sets `status: approved` and the approval fields. An agent writing them is a
contract violation." The three-rung approval ladder that permits an agent to transcribe a
chat approval — with this evidence file as the countersignature — is **proposed by the
very plan being approved here**. It is not yet in force.

So this approval is irregular by the letter of the contract it was taken under. The agent
wrote `status: approved`, `approved_by`, `approved_at`, and `plan_hash` into the plan
frontmatter on the strength of an unambiguous human instruction delivered in chat.

It is recorded here rather than smoothed over, for two reasons. First, the failure mode
this whole plan exists to fix is an approval whose frontmatter looks indistinguishable
from a properly executed one; suppressing this record would be an instance of exactly that
defect, committed while fixing it. Second, a future reader auditing the FolioOS history
will find one approved plan whose approval an agent wrote, and that reader deserves to
find the reason next to it rather than infer a rule violation.

**The bootstrap is intrinsic, not an oversight.** Any change to the approval procedure
must be approved under the procedure it replaces. The alternatives were to approve this
plan by the 0.3.0 method — the 64-character transcription the plan exists to eliminate,
from the device that cannot do it — or to not change the procedure. Neither is better than
approving it by the method it proposes and saying so.
